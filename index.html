What it does

Works on the venue’s own plan

The floor is the venue’s own dimension plan, calibrated so that a metre on screen is a metre in the hall. Stands are placed, rotated and reshaped in real metres, and aisle widths are measured against your minimum. The columns are drawn over everything, because a column hidden under a stand on the plan is a column discovered at bump‑in.

Prices what it draws

Stand fees follow the size sold, each stand carries its furniture allowance, and anything beyond it is charged at your hire rates. The fitout view, the furniture schedule and the CSV exports are worked from the same figures, so they cannot disagree about money.

Keeps the records

Exhibitor, supplier and staff directories live beside the plan. Allocate a stand to an exhibitor and the plan carries their name. Tick off applications, deposits and payments, and the running totals follow. Alternative layouts are saved as schemes of the same fair, so they all share the same directories.

Talks to your exhibitors

Each exhibitor gets a sheet of their own — itself a single file that opens anywhere — showing their stand, their included furniture and the hire list at your prices. They arrange their fitout on it and send it back; you import the file and confirm. Nothing is retyped, and anything they ask for beyond what they were sent is flagged when you load it.

One file is the whole point

Stand Command is a single HTML file. Open it in a browser and that is the tool. There is nothing to install, nothing to host, nothing to subscribe to, and no login between you and the plan. It works offline in the hall at bump‑in. Layouts save as plain files and reopen exactly as saved, so the file is the backup and a copy on a USB stick is the whole disaster plan. The sheets you send exhibitors are the same kind of file, so they open on whatever computer the exhibitor has.

See it working

The demo is a complete working copy of the tool, configured for the fictional Port Meridian fair — its own hall, its own furniture and price list, an example layout, the exhibitors on it, and the sheet one of them would receive. It goes to fair organisers by email, on request.
